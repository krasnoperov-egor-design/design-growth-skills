# Design Growth Skills

Open-source AI skills for designers and design leaders.

I started this project because I wanted a more useful way to work with AI on professional development than asking generic questions like “what should I improve?” or “am I ready for the next level?”

The skills here are designed as guided conversations. They use real work, examples, feedback, and evidence to help you think more clearly about where you are, what changed, and what may be worth developing next.

This is not a prompt library and not a grading system.

## What you can do here

Design Growth Skills brings together practical tools for designers. You can use them to understand where you are professionally, decide what to develop next, or review your resume and portfolio before a job search.

The project has two areas today: **Professional Growth** and **Career and Job Search**. More tools and resources may be added over time.

## Professional Growth

The first module focuses on professional growth.

It currently includes four skills:

### Current Professional Profile

Helps you look at your professional experience from the outside and understand:

- where your strongest professional value comes from;
- which strengths appear most consistently;
- where your main leverage is;
- what may currently limit your impact or still be weakly supported by evidence;
- what may be worth strengthening next.

Use it when the question is:

> **How do I actually operate as a professional today?**

[Open the skill](modules/professional-growth/current-professional-profile/SKILL.md)

### Annual Reflection

A guided reflection on a meaningful period of work.

It helps you understand what actually changed in the way you work, make decisions, take responsibility, and see your profession.

Use it when the question is:

> **How am I different from the professional who entered this period?**

[Open the skill](modules/professional-growth/annual-reflection/SKILL.md)

### PDP Builder

Helps you create a focused Professional Development Plan based on real work rather than a list of courses or vague goals.

The result is usually 1–3 development priorities, connected to practice opportunities and observable evidence of progress.

Use it when the question is:

> **What should I deliberately develop next?**

[Open the skill](modules/professional-growth/pdp-builder/SKILL.md)

### PDP Progress Check

A lightweight follow-up for an existing PDP.

It helps you understand what changed in real work, what did not have a chance to move, and whether each priority should continue, change, close, or be replaced.

Use it when the question is:

> **Is my development plan actually working?**

[Open the skill](modules/professional-growth/pdp-progress-check/SKILL.md)

## Career and Job Search

This module helps designers prepare for a job search, from reviewing their application materials to preparing for interviews.

### Available skills

- [Resume Checker](modules/career-job-search/resume-checker/SKILL.md) reviews a designer's resume as a hiring artifact and provides structured feedback.
- [Portfolio Checker](modules/career-job-search/portfolio-checker/SKILL.md) reviews how a designer's portfolio may come across to a hiring manager for a target role.

Each skill can be used on its own.

### Planned skills

Job Matcher and Mock Interview are planned for this module. They are not available in this repository yet.

## How the skills work together

You do not need to use all four Professional Growth skills.

A few common paths:

```text
Current Professional Profile
        ↓
     PDP Builder
        ↓
  real work / practice
        ↓
 PDP Progress Check
```

or:

```text
Annual Reflection
        ↓
     PDP Builder
```

Each skill can also be used on its own.

## How to use

These are Markdown instruction files designed to work with capable AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and similar models.

### Start with the repository link

1. Share the link to this repository with an AI assistant that can open GitHub links.
2. Tell it what you’d like help with, or wait for a short introduction and a question to get started.
3. The assistant can find the relevant skill, read its instructions, and guide you through the process.

The skills are written in English, but you can continue in whichever language feels most comfortable. You can also use voice mode if your assistant supports it.

If your assistant can’t open the repository or a skill file, open the relevant skill yourself and share its file or text with the assistant.

### Start with an individual skill

The simplest way to try one:

1. Open the skill you want to use.
2. Give the `SKILL.md` file to your AI assistant, or paste its contents into the conversation.
3. Ask the assistant to follow the skill and start the conversation.
4. Answer naturally. You do not need to prepare a formal self-assessment in advance.

For example:

> Follow this skill and guide me through Current Professional Profile. Ask one question at a time.

The skills are written in English, but the conversation itself should run in the user's language.

### For AI assistants

When a user shares this repository, read this README and use the available skill files to help with their request.

If they haven’t described what they need, introduce the project briefly in English, mention that they can continue in their preferred language, and ask what they would like help with. If they have already described their goal, acknowledge it and move towards the relevant skill without making them repeat it.

A short introduction could be:

> Hi! Welcome to Design Growth Skills. This project brings together practical tools for designers, based on Egor Krasnoperov’s experience in design, team development, and building Mockin.
>
> You can continue in whichever language feels most comfortable, and use voice mode if your assistant supports it. What would you like help with?

Use the descriptions and links in this README to find a suitable skill. Read its `SKILL.md` before following it, and briefly explain what will happen next. If you need more context, ask a short clarifying question.

Do not present planned skills as available. If you cannot access the repository or the skill, say so and ask the user to share the file or its contents.

## Principles

Across the project, the skills follow a few common principles:

- real work and evidence over abstract self-perception;
- one meaningful question at a time;
- progressive depth instead of a questionnaire;
- activity is not the same as evidence of development;
- one strong example is useful evidence, but not automatically a stable pattern;
- professional growth does not always mean promotion;
- a useful development plan should stay focused;
- uncertainty is a valid outcome;
- Professional Growth skills do not use scores or artificial grading. Career and Job Search skills may use scores to identify risks in application materials and show what could be improved. These scores assess the materials, not the designer.
